## STAGE 2.7 — SPATIAL / LOCATION ANALYSIS (v3.9; runs automatically in UNDERWRITE and FULL modes)

Auto-invokes `submarket-spatial-analysis` on every UNDERWRITE and FULL evaluation — no separate manual skill call. Trigger is mode-based (UNDERWRITE or FULL selected per ROUTING), independent of Stage 2.6's T1-PURSUE gate: a directly-requested "build the model" fires this stage even for a deal that never ran through SCREEN.

**Default workflows — both run every time, standard case, no elicitation form:**
1. **Acquisition Location Score (1–100)** — composite: highway distance (25%) + intermodal distance (20%) + 30-min drive-time population (15%) + 30-min drive-time labor pool (15%) + submarket vacancy/rent-growth trend (15%) + major-employer/anchor proximity (10%), per the spatial skill's own weighting.
2. **Last-Mile Suitability** — population-ring distances (5/10/15-mi), truck-court/dock-door inventory, highway access/signal density, per the spatial skill's own scorecard.

Do not surface the spatial skill's workflow-selection form for a standard UNDERWRITE/FULL run. Its other workflows (Submarket Comparison, Tenant Mix Optimization) stay opt-in — invoke only if Gabriel specifically asks for a cross-submarket comparison or an existing-property tenant-mix read.

**Sourcing discipline (inherited from the spatial skill, not re-derived here):** no live routing/Census API exists today. Every distance, population, and labor figure carries `[est]` unless independently verified against a live GIS/Census/OSRM pull, in which case it is tagged `CONFIRMED`. Forecasted demographic change stays `[FORECAST]`. Carry the spatial skill's stated Census ACS data lag (12–24 months) and drive-time assumptions (OSRM/Mapbox where available, stated explicitly) into the score memo unchanged.

**Scope boundary — do not conflate with the Stage 2 scorecard:** the Location Score composite and Last-Mile Suitability score are DD/context inputs for IC Memo §4 narrative, NOT a Stage 2 scorecard input and NOT a substitute for the existing Market & Location row (12 pts, cluster-adjacency-first — Stage 2 table row 4). This stage changes no scorecard weight.

**Output:** location-score memo (DOCX) + last-mile scorecard (XLSX) per the spatial skill's own naming convention; feeds IC MEMO STRUCTURE §4 and §12 below.

## STAGE 2.8 — FUNCTIONAL OBSOLESCENCE & DURABILITY GRADE (FORG v1.0 — PROPOSED, pilot before ratifying weights; runs on every FULL)

Runs on every FULL evaluation; **recommended, not yet mandatory, on SCREEN** when Stage 2.5 or Stage 2.7 flags material vintage, vacancy, or land-heavy characteristics. Produces two independent 0–100 scores — **Building Durability Score (BDS)** and **Land & Locational Offset Score (LOS)** — combined into a **Functional Obsolescence Risk Tier (FORT, A–D)**. Reported in the verdict block **and, since v4.33, applied to Basis points** (Stage 2 row 1: × 1.00 for FORT A, A–, B+, B, B– / × 0.75 for C+, C / × 0.50 for D+, D; Combined uses the adjusted Basis). Changes no Stage 1 floor, no walk price, no P(exec), no BDS/LOS factor weight, and no governor constant.

**Sequencing — this stage cannot join the FULL-mode parallel fan-out (see MODES/FULL):** LOS factor 3 imports Stage 2.7's Acquisition Location Score directly, so Stage 2.8 runs only after Stage 2.7 returns. Same sequencing logic that keeps Stage 2.6 out of the parallel batch, though the reason differs — here the dependency is a required input, not a walk-price invalidation risk.

**Part A — Tenant-Class Fit (gates which BDS-1/BDS-2 threshold table applies).** Classify the building's realistic FUTURE tenant pool, not its current occupant: **National/International Distribution** (excluded — outside the buy-box per STAGE 0 Gate 2's ≤50,000 SF / $500K–$10M size-price bounds), **Local/Regional Distribution**, or **Light Industrial / Local Trade** (ORA's core). Score BDS-1 and BDS-2 against the light-industrial/local-regional table unless size and submarket genuinely place the asset in the local/regional-distribution pool. **Never score against a national/international bulk standard.** [PRIMARY] Prologis Research (2015/updated 2025-12-07) shows obsolescence pace is tenant-type-dependent: national/international distributors chase maximum spec and their buildings age fastest; light-industrial/local-trade tenants are location-driven rather than spec-driven and obsolesce slowest. ORA's buy-box sits in that slowest tier by construction.

**Part B — Building Durability Score (BDS, 100 pts):**

| # | Factor | Pts | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear Height Adequacy | 15 | Scored tenant-class-relative. [PRIMARY] CBRE Research (2026-03-24) 14–28' shallow-bay band is the durable standard — do NOT penalize for falling short of bulk heights (32'+); a building is not obsolete because a bulk tenant wouldn't take it |
| 2 | Loading Configuration & Reversibility | 15 | Door mix, dock ratio, truck court depth against tenant class — use ORA's existing **100' ideal** truck-court convention (Stage 2 clear/loading matrix), NOT the bulk 130–185' figure. Grade↔dock conversion scores as LOW reversibility |
| 3 | Structural Flexibility (column spacing + floor slab) | 12 | **≥150 psf** slab = full credit. AMR-class automation is materially more tolerant of legacy column grids than fixed AS/RS — do not over-penalize tight bays on an automation theory |
| 4 | Power / Electrical Headroom | 12 | **[SPECULATIVE — no public numeric standard exists]** qualitative 3-tier score (adequate / marginal / constrained), pending the DD electrical read below; tier set per the **PAS role rule (v4.33)** below |
| 5 | Fire/Life-Safety (ESFR) Adequacy & Cure Cost | 10 | RCT v03's **+$4/SF** ESFR adder is a FLOOR, not a ceiling, whenever water supply (fire pump / storage tank) is unconfirmed |
| 6 | Divisibility / Reconfigurability | 12 | Redemising flexibility — a distinct durability hedge from the upside Stage 2 row 7 already scores |
| 7 | Site & Yard Functionality | 12 | The durability-hedge framing of the same facts Stage 2 row 7 scores as upside — since v4.33 the FORT tier scales Basis points, so these facts reach Combined twice in different forms — Stage 2 row 7 scores the upside, and BDS-7 only moves the tier (one input of eight, and a tier band step at most). Accepted as bounded; say so explicitly in the memo |
| 8 | Construction Type & Structural Longevity | 12 | Extends the existing block/tilt-up/brick full-credit, **PEMB ≈ half-credit** convention (an unknown construction type scores as PEMB) with a forward retrofit-ceiling view |

**Part C — Land & Locational Offset Score (LOS, 100 pts):**

| # | Factor | Pts | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land Value Share of Basis | 25 | Benchmark: [PRIMARY] MIT CRE thesis (She, 2020, advised by David Geltner) — national average redevelopment option value ≈ **19%** of price/SF-land. **≥40%** land share of basis = full credit |
| 2 | Redevelopment / HBU Optionality | 20 | Zoning flexibility, FAR headroom, and **observed** — not theoretical — alternative-use activity in the immediate area |
| 3 | Locational Durability | 25 | **Imports STAGE 2.7's Acquisition Location Score (1–100) directly — do not re-derive it.** Overlay infill-vs-greenfield per Prologis's "growth of cities" finding |
| 4 | Market Supply-Barrier Profile | 15 | Generalizes the existing **OBSOLESCENCE-WATCH** flag (Columbus/Indianapolis, plus Memphis per Prologis) into a scored factor across ALL markets — retain the flag's existing two-sided risk/tailwind framing (competing subdivided bulk supply vs. bulk-to-light-industrial demand migration) |
| 5 | Alternative-Use Exit Liquidity | 15 | Depth of the land/redevelopment buyer pool. The existing Delmarva thin-exit-pool caution (local-only buyer duopoly, 7–10 yr hold underwrite) is the standing low-credit archetype |

**Part D — FORT matrix.** Bands: BDS High **≥70** / Moderate **50–69** / Low **<50** · LOS High **≥60** / Moderate **35–59** / Low **<35**.

| BDS ↓ / LOS → | High | Moderate | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High** | **A** — Durable Core | **A–** — Durable, Adequate Cushion | **B+** — Spec-Durable, Land-Light |
| **Moderate** | **B** — Land-Carried Upside | **B–** — Balanced / Watch | **C+** — Exposed |
| **Low** | **C** — Land-Carried Core | **D+** — Elevated Risk | **D** — Melting Ice Cube |

**Required posture by tier — FORT is a posture, not just a label.** **A / A–:** generational hold is safe on building fundamentals alone; land offset is a bonus, not a load-bearing assumption. **B+:** fine while the building thesis holds — there is no land bailout if it doesn't; monitor tenant-mix drift and do NOT lean on land value in the pre-mortem. **B / B–:** state explicitly in the IC memo which axis is doing the work, since neither is strong enough alone to carry the hold thesis. **C+:** moderate building risk with no land cushion — requires an explicit compensating yield premium above the Stage 1 YoC floor, or a documented reason to proceed; log as an Exception Register item. **C:** the land-carried case, underwritten honestly — cap the building's expected functional life *inside* the hold horizon, size current yield to the building write-down, and verify the land thesis is evidenced rather than assumed. **D+:** the building likely fails functionally within the hold and the land cushion is only partial — requires BOTH a yield premium and a credible, evidenced land thesis. **D:** see Reporting below.

**20/30-year forward stress test (functional-obsolescence pre-mortem).** Run alongside the existing mandatory IC-memo pre-mortem (IC MEMO STRUCTURE §8) — one forward-dated question per BDS factor, asked against the Part A realistic tenant pool, never an aspirational one. **Clear height:** if the submarket's median tenant needs materially more cubic volume by 2046, does this building still clear the light-industrial/local-regional bar, or has the tenant mix itself moved? · **Loading:** if last-mile fleet composition changes (van size, EV range, delivery cadence), does the current door mix and truck court still function without site work this property physically cannot accommodate? · **Structural flexibility:** if even small-bay tenants adopt AMR-class mobile automation over the hold, does the floor/column grid support it without a slab replacement that would itself trigger a basis re-underwrite? · **Power:** if EV last-mile fleets or light automation become the dominant regional tenant profile by 2040, can this property's electrical service be upgraded at a cost that doesn't erase the deal's basis discount? · **Fire/life-safety:** does a future tenant rack-height increase push the building past ESFR's ceiling envelope, and is that discoverable and priced before it becomes a lease-renewal blocker? · **Divisibility / site & yard / construction type:** if the current tenant mix fails entirely, can this specific asset be re-let, redemised, or converted to an alternative use within the LOS-2/LOS-5 *evidenced* optionality?

**Reporting.** Report FORT in the verdict block alongside CARRY-FLAGGED / ENV-WATCH / LTV-FLAGGED / OBSOLESCENCE-WATCH. **A FORT-D verdict requires Gabriel's explicit sign-off before LOI** — treat it like the ratified portfolio-LTV governor, NOT a standard logged Exception Register item. FORT A / A– / B+ / B / B– / C+ / C proceed under Ruling 5 (flag, not kill) with a logged compensating factor as usual.

**New DD item (recommended — Stage 2.5, or as a Stage 2.8 sub-item):** electrical service size + utility-feed capacity check, for any deal where EV-fleet or automation-adjacent tenancy is plausible over the hold. This is a genuine, currently unscored data gap — not a duplicate of an existing Stage 2.5 item — and it is what resolves BDS-4's [SPECULATIVE] tag on a given deal.

## STAGE 2.9 — ALTERNATIVE ASSET CLASS TRACK: LAND / REDEVELOPMENT VALUE (v4.6 — PROPOSED, pilot before ratifying)

**Trigger:** fires when STAGE 0 Gate 6 flags automotive use, retail-only, or office-only (or Gabriel directly requests it), and/or STAGE 2.8's LOS-1 (Land Value Share of Basis) scores ≥40% land share. Runs **in addition to, never instead of**, the standard STAGE 1 floors and STAGE 2 scorecard — Ruling 5's flag-not-kill discipline and the existing Basis/Yield floors remain the deal's binding governing test until this track is separately ratified. **Why this exists:** the Stage 1 Basis/Yield floors are calibrated to shallow-bay industrial replacement cost (RCT v03) — running a genuinely different asset class (auto dealership, retail pad, land-banked redevelopment site) through that ruler is informative but strained. Direct product of the 2026-07-27 Pike Plaza portfolio underwrite (three automotive-dealership parcels, Indianapolis IN; see IC memo §10, "measuring a different asset class" finding).

**Sequencing — excluded from the FULL-mode parallel fan-out, same reason as Stage 2.8:** this stage imports STAGE 2.8's LOS-1 and LOS-2 directly, so it runs only after Stage 2.8 returns, and only when triggered.

**Three components, reported as a standing ALT-ASSET-TRACK block, never folded into Combined:**

| # | Component | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Land Value Share of Basis | **Imports STAGE 2.8 LOS-1 directly — do not re-derive.** ≥40% land share (LOS-1's full-credit threshold) is the trigger-confirming signal that a redevelopment-value read is warranted at all; report the LOS-1 score and underlying % here. |
| 2 | HBU (Highest & Best Use) Optionality | **Imports STAGE 2.8 LOS-2 directly — do not re-derive.** Zoning flexibility, FAR headroom, and OBSERVED — not theoretical — alternative-use activity nearby. |
| 3 | Comp-based valuation (NEW) | The genuinely new component — neither Stage 1 nor Stage 2.8 prices the asset off comps. Value the subject off **executed sale comps for the same specific use class** (auto dealership / retail pad / land-banked site, as applicable) and their market cap rates, NOT the RCT v03 discount-to-replacement test. |

**Comp-based valuation mechanics:** (a) pull ≥3 executed comps (per **COMP GRID DISCIPLINE**, `references/comp-grid-discipline.md`) for the same use class within the submarket or nearest comparable corridor; (b) derive a comp-implied cap rate band (LOW/POINT/HIGH) from those transactions' NOI (or imputed market rent if vacant/owner-user) ÷ price; (c) value = subject stabilized/market NOI ÷ comp-implied cap rate; (d) report **price-to-comp-value** as the redevelopment-track's Basis-analog metric, parallel to — never substituting for — the standard RCT discount-to-replacement. **Sourcing discipline:** comps must be executed transactions, not asking prices; broker-quoted "cap rates" without a disclosed NOI are directional-only, tagged `[est]`, same discipline as the Stage 2.1 vendor-report rule.

**Reporting, not scoring:** report a distinct **ALT-ASSET-TRACK** verdict flag in the verdict block (alongside FORT, CARRY-FLAGGED, etc.) summarizing LOS-1 %, LOS-2 read, comp-implied cap rate band, and price-to-comp-value. This is a **second lens** alongside the standard industrial-calibrated verdict — it does not override or replace the Stage 1 Basis/Yield floor verdict, which remains governing for gate/floor/flag purposes under Ruling 5 until this track is separately ratified. State both reads side by side in any IC memo or screen where this track fires — extend IC MEMO STRUCTURE §10 (Replacement Cost Analysis) to include the ALT-ASSET-TRACK comp-based read when this stage fires.

**PILOT BEFORE RATIFYING — same discipline as Stage 2.8.** Run manually on the next 3–5 SCREEN/FULL candidates carrying the trigger condition and log the comp-implied cap rate, price-to-comp-value, and LOS-1/LOS-2 reads to the Deal Sourcing Log before treating any of this as scored or binding. **No scorecard weight, Stage 1 floor, or governor constant changes as a result of this stage** — it is purely additive, a second read for a genuinely different asset class, not a recalibration of the existing one. **Validation:** Pike Plaza #1/#2/#3 (5051/5155/5309 Pike Plaza Rd, Indianapolis IN — three automotive-dealership parcels underwritten 2026-07-27; all three cleared Gate 6 as an exception per Ruling 5 and were scored against the standard industrial floors, producing an honest PASS-band verdict despite genuine locational/redevelopment merit). This stage is the direct response to that finding, intended to make the next automotive/retail deal scoreable on its own terms alongside, not instead of, the standard read.
